For my final project, I want to compare how the different chickadee species populations of the US have changed over time and compare that to climate change related data (temperatures, storms, etc.). I also want to compare the different species to eachother.

I have downloaded a large dataset from The Institute for Bird Populations MAPS Data Exploration Tool (https://ibp-maps-data-exploration-tool.org/app/maps). This dataset includes data of five different Chickadee species (BCCH, BOCH, CACH, CBCH, MOCH) collected for the Monitoring Avian Productivity and Survivorship (MAPS) program between 1992-05-01 to 2018-08-12. There are 57414 rows (each a separate entry of a captured chickadee) and 48 columns (each containing different data collected about the chickadee encounter). 

One of my main interests is to see how each of the different chickadee species populations have responded to raising temperatures over time and how their population ranges have changed. I expect the ranges to move northward and up in elevation. Here is a promising dataset for average monthly temperatures by US state: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/justinrwong/average-monthly-temperature-by-us-state. I have also found an online interactive land cover model from https://apps.fs.usda.gov/lcms-viewer/ that could prove to be useful. 

Questions I could answer:
* How have the different chickadee ranges changed over time and in relation to one another? How does this relate to climate change (global temperatures, land cover changes)? How have the hybridization zones between the different chickadee species changed?
* How have chickadee populations changed over time (weight, wing length, fat content, population age content)?
* Could analyze survivorship (recaptures of banded birds).
